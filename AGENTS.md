# Mavern brand, copy, and advertising guide

Use this guide for Mavern pages, campaigns, social posts, and other public material. [Mavern.md](Mavern.md) is the source of truth for what Mavern is and why it exists.

## The brand in one sentence

Mavern is an independent creative practice spanning development, art, music, writing, and experimentation. Creativity is the objective. Keep its public presence thoughtful, direct, and made with care.

Keep the work human and specific. Let the quality of the idea, words, and details carry the brand. Do not add polish, decoration, or claims just to make Mavern look larger than it is.

## Start with what is true

Mavern is an umbrella for creative work, not a single product. The offer, audience, and facts belong to each project and must be established before writing claims about that project. Do not guess what a release does, who it serves, how long it has operated, or what results it gets.

Before writing a campaign or a page that makes a project or business claim, establish:

- What is being offered, in plain words.
- Who it is for, when that matters to the work.
- What someone can do, experience, or understand through it.
- What is distinctly Mavern or project-specific about the work.
- What proof supports each factual or comparative claim.
- What the reader should do next.

If a missing fact changes the message, ask for it. Keep drafts neutral while it is unknown. Never fill gaps with invented customer stories, results, numbers, history, awards, availability, or testimonials.

## Voice and wording

- Write plainly, with familiar words and active verbs.
- Be confident without boasting. Be warm without forced friendliness.
- Prefer a concrete detail over an abstract promise. Name the work, object, process, decision, or result the reader can picture.
- For project copy, explain the work and its actual purpose. Where an audience or practical need matters, name it plainly.
- Keep one main thought in each sentence and one main promise in each section or ad.
- Use short headings that tell the reader what follows. Keep headings in sentence case.
- Make buttons and links name their action: “See the work,” “View the details,” or “Get in touch.”
- Keep personality in the phrasing, not in a layer of puns, slogans, or filler.

Avoid stock marketing phrases such as “unlock your potential,” “seamless solutions,” “next-level,” “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “innovative solutions,” and “built for the future.” Avoid calling Mavern “premium,” “authentic,” or “world-class” unless a specific, useful fact explains what that means.

### A useful copy shape

For a project release or creative work, explain what it is, what led to it, and how someone can experience it. For a service or practical offer, name the situation it addresses, what it does, the evidence behind it, and the next step. Use only the parts that help the reader; this is a guide, not a fixed formula.

## Brand strategy

- Build positioning from real customer needs, the actual offer, and evidence. Do not choose a slogan first and invent a business around it.
- Make the difference easy to explain in one sentence. If it relies on vague adjectives, find a more concrete distinction.
- Tie Mavern's story to real choices, people, materials, process, or history. Do not invent a founder mythology or claim a tradition that does not exist.
- Build recognition by using Mavern's own mark, colors, type, and recurring visual details consistently. Give these elements room to become familiar over time.
- Keep each page and campaign recognisably Mavern while adapting its format to the channel and purpose.

## Visual direction

The current Mavern site establishes a useful starting palette: warm paper `#f7f6f2`, deep green `#253d30`, charcoal `#21231f`, and muted green `#77906e`. The samples in `assets/samples/` explore forest green, terracotta, and cream logo versions. Treat those files as studies until a version is explicitly selected. A Mavern-wide visual system should identify work as part of the umbrella while allowing each project a fitting visual language.

- Use a restrained palette. Keep text easy to read and reserve accent colors for a clear purpose.
- Pair expressive display type with comfortable, legible body type. Preserve the calm editorial character already present in the site.
- Give the main idea room. Use deliberate alignment, spacing, and image crops instead of filling every open area.
- Prefer real Mavern work, people, tools, places, materials, and process as visual evidence when available.
- Use illustration or generated imagery to express a concept, not to imply that a product, customer, location, or result exists when it does not.
- Keep the logo's shape and proportions intact. Do not add effects, redraw it, or silently replace the existing mark. Ask before treating a sample as the selected identity.
- Keep motion short and purposeful. The page must still communicate clearly with motion disabled and must respect reduced-motion settings.
- Make every design work at phone size, with readable text, usable controls, visible focus, and sufficient contrast.

Avoid interchangeable landing-page structures, decorative gradients without meaning, arbitrary floating elements, empty feature grids, false interface mockups, and visual effects that compete with the message. Do not add a section, illustration, or animation solely to make a page look fuller.

## Advertising guide

Before developing an ad, write a short brief with the audience, the moment or need, the offer, the single message, the proof, the placement, and the intended action. If any of these are unknown, mark them as unknown instead of inventing them.

- Give each ad one clear job: introduce Mavern, make a real benefit memorable, explain an offer, or prompt a specific action.
- Put the offer or useful idea where it can be understood quickly. Make the brand visible early enough that the message can be remembered as Mavern's.
- Use an ownable Mavern cue—mark, palette, type, language, or a recurring visual detail—and keep it consistent across a campaign.
- Show the actual work or a believable use situation. Avoid generic stock scenes and fabricated interface or product images presented as real evidence.
- Keep the headline, visual, and call to action on the same idea. Send the reader to a page that continues that idea and clearly explains the offer.
- Make versions for different placements by changing the composition and amount of copy, not by shrinking one crowded design into every size.
- Test distinct messages or ideas when useful. Do not call a result successful without observing a real outcome and stating what was measured.
- Do not invent scarcity, countdowns, discounts, customer endorsements, or guarantees. State important conditions plainly.

For every objective claim, keep a note of its source and confirm it is current before publication. Claims about performance, price, availability, comparisons, customer results, endorsements, or environmental impact need clear evidence. Do not use a disclaimer to rescue a headline that gives the wrong impression. Get the owner's review of public claims and locally applicable advertising requirements before releasing an ad.

## Finish and review

Before handing off a page or campaign, check that:

- The reader can tell what is offered, who it is for, and what to do next.
- Each claim has a source, or has been removed or marked for confirmation.
- The wording sounds natural when read aloud and has no filler or unexplained jargon.
- The visual has a clear focal point and uses Mavern's own identity consistently.
- The small-screen version remains legible and complete.
- Any imagery, testimonial, quote, logo, or customer reference is real and approved for use.

## Development principles

- Keep files focused. Split pages, sections, content, styles, and reusable behavior into clear modules instead of growing one all-purpose file. Avoid master files that run into thousands of lines.
- Keep page-specific content close to its page and shared design tokens or components in an obvious shared location. Do not create abstractions until they remove real repetition or make a likely next addition easier.
- Build responsive layouts mobile first: start with the narrow-screen content order and controls, then add wider-screen composition with breakpoints. Do not treat the desktop layout as the source that gets squeezed down.
- Make navigation, content types, and project listings easy to extend. Adding a new project or medium should usually mean adding content and its assets, not rewriting the site structure.
- Prefer semantic HTML, accessible controls, descriptive names, and small components with clear responsibilities. Keep interactions understandable without relying on animation.
- Before adding a dependency or framework, check whether the existing stack already handles the need. Keep the runtime and maintenance burden proportional to the work.
- Keep project-specific presentation flexible within Mavern's shared identity. A new game, essay, song, or software release may need a different layout and media treatment.
- Leave code easier to understand and extend than you found it. Remove dead paths and stale placeholders when touching the area; do not spread temporary decisions into shared foundations.
